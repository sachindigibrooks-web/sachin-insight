Booking your first deep tissue massage? Here's what to expect.

Deep tissue uses slow, firm pressure to reach the deeper layers of muscle that a lighter massage can't. Tight areas can feel intense, but intense and painful are two different things.

If you're holding your breath or tensing up, the pressure is too much. Tell your therapist in the moment. Pressure can change within a session.

Mild soreness for a day or two afterward is normal, a bit like after a hard workout. Water, gentle movement and a warm shower can help.

## LinkedIn (1200×627)
**Caption**

"Deeper is better" is one of the most common assumptions people bring to their first deep tissue massage.

In practice, the right pressure matters more than the most pressure. When pressure goes past what your body can handle, you tense up and hold your breath. A good session is intense in tight areas without tipping into pain, and the pressure shifts with the area being worked.

The same principle applies to most performance work: steady, well-judged effort beats maximum force.

What we tell first-time clients:
• Share what your body needs before the session
• Speak up in the moment rather than pushing through
• Expect mild soreness for a day or two, and tell us if it lasts longer
