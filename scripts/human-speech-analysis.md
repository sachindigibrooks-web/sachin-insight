# What makes a voiceover sound human

Notes on why AI voiceovers sound robotic and how real people talk, used to rewrite the SEO trends script (`seo-social-trends-oct-2026.md`, v2).

## Why v1 sounded robotic

| v1 (written-style) | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Here's something most brands still miss." | Sounds like a blog intro, not someone talking |
| "In 2026, social media *is* a search engine." | Perfect grammar, no filler, no breath |
| "One: social SEO. Two: search everywhere. Three: be real." | List read out like slides |
| Every sentence the same length | Same rhythm all the way through = monotone |
| No reactions | A real person laughs, sighs and reacts to what they're saying |

## How people actually talk

1. **Fillers and openers:** "okay so", "like", "honestly", "yeah", "kinda". People buy time to think.
2. **Contractions and casual words:** "wanna", "it's", "don't", "kinda wild". Nobody says "do not" in a Reel.
3. **Questions to the listener:** "Where do you actually look first? Google? ...probably not, right?" This turns the audio into a conversation.
4. **Broken sentences and fragments:** "Most people? They're on TikTok. YouTube. Reddit." Spoken English is choppy.
5. **Self-interruptions and asides:** "Oh — and bonus tip." People remember things mid-sentence.
6. **Mixed sentence length and pace:** short punchy lines, then a longer explaining one. Speed up when excited, slow down when making a point.
7. **Pauses in odd places:** "...the answer? It's pulling from..." A pause before the key word builds tension.
8. **Small reactions:** a chuckle, a soft laugh, a sigh. They're tiny, but they're what makes the audio sound human.
9. **Emotion shifts:** curious → excited → thoughtful → warm. A real person's mood changes across 60 seconds.
10. **Stressing only 1–2 words per idea:** "People are SO tired of AI stuff."
11. **Personal sign-off:** "I post this stuff every week" instead of "Follow for more SEO trends".

## How this maps to ElevenLabs (eleven_v4)

- `...` (ellipsis) → thinking pause
- `—` (em dash) → short beat or self-interruption
- `[short pause]` → deliberate pause before a reveal
- `[chuckles]`, `[laughs softly]`, `[sighs]` → small human reactions (use 3–4 per minute max, or it sounds fake)
- `[casually]`, `[curious]`, `[excited]`, `[thoughtfully]`, `[warmly]`, `[upbeat]` → change only where the mood actually changes
- CAPS on one word → emphasis ("SO tired")
- Voice: "Matt – Natural, Authentic, Engaging" (a conversational social-media voice)

## Script v2 (human version)

[casually] Okay, so... quick question. When you wanna buy something — like, a new pair of shoes — where do you actually look first? [short pause] Google? [chuckles] Yeah... probably not anymore, right?

[curious] Most people? They're on TikTok. YouTube. Reddit. Honestly, social media is basically a search engine now. So — here's what's working in SEO right now. Three things. Real quick.

[excited] First one. Social SEO. Say your main keyword in, like, the first three seconds. Out loud. Put it on screen, put it in the caption... [laughs softly] because yeah, the algorithm's literally listening to you now.

[thoughtfully] Second... and this one's kinda wild. When you ask ChatGPT or Google's AI something — the answer? It's pulling from YouTube videos, Reddit threads, TikToks. So if you're not posting there... [short pause] AI just doesn't know you exist.

[warmly] And third — just be real. People are SO tired of AI-generated stuff. Show your face, tell your story, actually reply to comments. That's what gets saved... and shared.

[casually] Oh — and bonus tip. Don't just post one video. Make it a series. Part one, part two... people come back for that.

[sighs] So yeah. Google still matters, obviously. But show up wherever your customer's scrolling. [upbeat] Follow for more — I post this stuff every week.
